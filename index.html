Urban drainage systems undergo a lot of pressure when dealing with heavy rainfall, this can result in water flooding and cause disruption to day-to-day life. Green roofs, which combine rainwater management, human wellbeing, and sustainability, are an emerging solution to surface water flooding. Data collected represents how significantly green roofs retain rainfall and decrease building electrical usage which, in result, decrease bills. These findings implicate that green roofs have a positive effect on quality of life by preventing flooding and having financial benefits. In addition, green roofs are a long term investment as they have an average life span of 45 years before needing a refresh, furthermore, their downsides become minor when properly maintained. Therefore, determining that green roofs considerably improve quality of life and reduce water flooding in a densely urbanised environment.

Urban flooding tends to be the result of limited areas of open soil for water storage in cities and towns. The damage caused by urban flooding disrupts developing and developed societies as it causes immense damage to various facilities, which results in loss of employment and disruptions to transport systems - these components considerably decrease quality of life. Therefore, it is important to innovate new ways to prevent urban flooding. This article will discuss one method to prevent urban flooding - green roofs. It will then evaluate the extent to which green roofs can reduce surface water flooding in a densely ubranised environments whilst improving quality of life. (Jhe, Bloch and Lamond, 2012, p.57)

Firstly, it is essential to understand the structure of a typical green roof and how each layer contributes to the confunction of a green roof. Green roofs are flat or slightly sloped structures that support vegetation and allow water to drain through. They function by retaining up to 75% of rainfall, helping to reduce rooftop water runoff and prevent problems such as urban flooding and sewer overflows. Green roofs consist of unique multifunctional layers. The vegetation layer depends on the local climate; the growing medium provides the essential nutrients that plants need to grow. The waterproof membrane protects the building from rainfall and moisture as water seepage could cause issues such as mould and a weakened structure, potentially leading to the roof collapsing. Finally, structural support is necessary as the green roofs add considerable weight to the roof deck. All these components offer a secure way to prevent urban flooding without affecting how the building functions. (Sika Ireland, n.d.)(UK Flat Roofing, n.d.-a)

The combination of green roofs and sustainable drainage systems allows these systems to decrease stormwater runoff, reducing the burden on urban drainage networks by capturing rainwater that would otherwise flow across impermeable surfaces. Furthermore, green roofs trap sediments which allows them to act as a filter for drainage systems. During rainstorms, delaying water flow can immensely help manage the volume of water for drainage crew. As the concentration and pressure of rainwater is significantly lifted from urban drainage systems, the flooding on access routes is minimised which allows the crew to efficiently and safely reach sites to handle water volume, before the systems are overwhelmed. This prevents urban areas from flooding. Collectively, these points highlight how much green roofs can help with reducing surface water flooding in densely urbanised areas. (Livingroofs.org, n.d.)

Green roofs affect more than just water retention. For example, schools across Spain and Portugal had indoor temperatures drop by 4°C to 6°C, and cooling-related electricity consumption was reduced by over 11%. By cutting down building energy use, these roofs contribute to climate control and oppose the effects of global warming. They also cool down cities by providing shade and releasing moisture via transpiration. Green roofs enhance water quality by filtering pollutants in rainwater, thereby reducing contamination and as a result improving the public health. The installation of green roofs enhances air quality which results in improved health of the public. Furthermore, these roofs also boost quality of life by improving well-being as they provide calming green spaces and increase healthy food production which encouraes better consumer diet by adding variety to produce products. (Directorate-General for Environment, 2026)(Sika Ireland, n.d.)(Luckett, 2025)(Intermountain Roofscapes, 2024a)(U.S. Environment Protection Agency, 2026)

Green roofs may require structural reinforcement due to their weight and also require more maintenance than conventional roofs as they need weeding and watering during periods of dry weather - this increases their expenses. Furthemore, green roofs require regular drainage as they deal with large volumes of water - without drainage they may become damaged over time. In some cases, they can become overwhelmed with water during heavy rainstorms which could cause leaks or water damage. However, adding a blue-green detention layer would be something to consider, especially if the region in which the green roof is built in is prone to heavy rainfall, as this layer holds up to 80 litres of water per square metre and then releases this water over 24-72 hours. Although green roofs typically involve higher upfront installation costs than conventional roofs, their long-term benefits outweigh the initial investment. They also have an average life span of 45 years, so they are long-term investments. Overall, with maintenance and careful planning, green roof disadvantages are insignificant - particulary when compared to the advantages. (The Flood Hub, 2022)(Intermountain Roofscapes, 2024b)(UK Flat Roofing, n.d.-b)(Zinco Green Roof Systems UK, n.d.)

To conclude, green roofs help reduce the pressure on urban drainage systems and therefore also reduce the surface water flooding in densely urbanised environment. They do this by absorbing rainwater, and in cases where they get overwhelmed, they can store it in a layer which releases the water over a period of time. Green roofs also increase quality of life by providing green spaces. improving air quality and encouraging greener diets. Their disadvantages, are minimal considering that the green roofs are maintained and are equipped for heavy rainfall. Therefore green roofs substantially improve quality of life and reduce surface water flooding in a densley urbanised environment.
