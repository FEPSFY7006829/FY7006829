Urban flooding tends to be the result of limited areas of open soil for water storage in cities ad towns. The damage caused by urban flooding disrupts both developed and developing societies as it causes immense damage to various facilities which results in loss of employment and disruptions to transport systems. Therefore, it is important to innovate new ways to prevent urban flooding. This article will discuss one method to prevent urban flooding - green roofs. It will then evaluate the extent to which green roofs can reduce surface watwr flooding in densely ubranised environments. (Jhe, Bloch and Lamond, 2012, p.57)

Green roofs are generally flat or slightly sloped structures that support vegetation and allow water to drain through. They function by retaining up to 75% of rainfall, helping to reduce rooftop water runoff and prevent problems such as urban flooding and sewer overflows. Green roofs consist of unique multifunctional layers. The vegetation layer entirely depends on the local climate; the growing medium provides the essential nutrients that plants need to grow. The waterproof membrane protects the building from rainfall and moisture, as water seepage could cause several issues such as mould and a weakened structure, potentially leading to the roof collapsing. Finally, structural support is necessary as green roofs add considerable weight to the roof deck. All these components offer a secure way to prevent urban flooding without affecting how the building functions, as the design behind green roofs is fully functioning and does not cause any disruptions (Sika Ireland, n.d.; UK Flat Roofing, n.d.).

Green roofs affect more than just water retention. For example, schools across Spain and Portugal had indoor temperatures drop by 4°C to 6°C, and cooling-related electricity consumption was reduced by over 11%. Additionally, these systems significantly decreased stormwater runoff, reducing the burden on urban drainage networks by capturing rainwater that would otherwise flow across impermeable surfaces. Moreover, these roofs can further boost quality of life by improving well-being, as they provide calming green spaces. The combination of green roofs and Sustainable Drainage Systems allows large stormwater volumes to place less pressure on drainage systems, as green roofs reduce and delay water runoff. Green roofs also enhance water quality by filtering pollutants from rainwater, thereby reducing contamination and minimising maintenance costs (Directorate-General for Environment, 2026; Sika Ireland, n.d.; Luckett, 2025).
